## Introduction

Vembanad Lake, Kerala's largest Ramsar wetland, is a lifeline for the region. It nurtures diverse wildlife, sustains local communities through fishing and farming, and serves as a crucial waterway for transportation. Yet, its rich ecosystem faces a growing threat from invasive water hyacinth, making effective monitoring and management more important than ever.

## Aim and Objectives

**Aim:** to find changes in water hyacinth growth in Vembanad Lake and provide actionable insights for ecological management.

**Objectives:**

- To evaluate the feasibility of mapping water hyacinth growth in Vembanad Lake using Sentinel-2 satellite imagery.
- To analyze seasonal trends and identify any significant spikes in water hyacinth growth during the summer season.
- To utilize NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) to map the locations where water hyacinth is most prevalent in the lake.

## Study Site

Vembanad Lake, located in the central part of Kerala, India. Coordinates: 9.5968° N, 76.3985° E.

## Methods

**Objective 1:** Used Sentinel-2 imagery to calculate NDVI and analyze surface reflectance (B2 vs. B11) to identify and classify water hyacinth growth.

**Objective 2:** Conducted seasonal NDVI analysis and created a Delta NDVI map to detect and highlight spikes in water hyacinth growth during summer.

**Objective 3:** Mapped high-density vegetation zones using NDVI thresholds, validated results with field data, and confirmed hotspots using spectral reflectance analysis.

## Discussion and Conclusion

The study demonstrates the effectiveness of Sentinel-2 satellite imagery in monitoring water hyacinth growth in Vembanad Lake. NDVI and Delta NDVI analyses, along with spectral reflectance (B2 vs. B11), effectively mapped vegetation changes and distinguished water hyacinth from other features. Seasonal analysis revealed a spike in growth during summer due to reduced water flow and increased sunlight, with significant proliferation near the Thanneermukkom Bund. Key hotspots were identified, but limitations like atmospheric distortions require improved methods. Conservation strategies include mechanical removal, promoting native vegetation, biocontrol through natural pests, and integrating remote sensing with community-led efforts for sustainable lake management.
